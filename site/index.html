Peer allocation of credit

The people doing the work decide who gets paid.

Bounties pay for work you can specify in advance. Communities run on work you cannot specify in advance: hosting, moderating, mentoring, showing up. FairShare lets a group fund that work together. A Sputnik DAO funds a round, members give each other points for the work they saw, and the contract settles payouts onchain. Every payout can be explained, giver by giver.

How it works

1

Fund a round

A Sputnik DAO funds a round with one envelope. The DAO votes, the proposal executes, and the envelope sits in escrow in the contract.

2

Give points

Every member gets 100 points and gives them to at least 3 other members, with a short reason for each gift. You cannot point at yourself, and you cannot spend points you do not have.

3

Settle onchain

The contract turns points into payouts. A score rewards breadth: points from many distinct givers count for more than the same points from one giver. Mutual giving is discounted by half, and nobody takes more than 25% of the envelope.

4

Explain every payout

Anyone can ask the contract why a member was paid what they were paid, giver by giver, discount by discount. No hidden inputs.

Round rules: 100 points per member · at least 3 recipients · cap 25% of the envelope · reciprocity discount 50% · giver weight is standing, starting at 100 · a round lasts 7 days unless the owner closes it early. Settling also mints ownership units in proportion to each payout, and older units fade by 10% each round. When rental income lands in the treasury, it is shared out in proportion to units. Ownership grows out of work, not out of buying in.

The maths in one line: score equals the square of the sum, over distinct givers, of the square root of effective points times giver weight. The same 100 points arriving from 5 givers scores 48400. The same 100 points arriving from 2 givers scores 19600. Breadth wins.

The live flow

  1. Connect a NEAR wallet. Testnet only. The wallet connection is not wired in this build, so this step cannot run from this page yet. This is stated plainly, not hidden.
  2. Create and fund a round. A Sputnik DAO proposes a FunctionCall to create_round with the envelope attached. Only the owner or the DAO can call it. The DAO votes, and the proposal executes.
  3. Allocate. Each member calls allocate with their points and reasons. One submission per member; sending again replaces the old one.
  4. Settle. Anyone calls settle_round once the 7 day deadline passes or the owner closes the round early. The contract pays each member from the envelope and the round closes.

Honest limits

  1. Peer allocation is not new. Coordinape did a version of it, and square root weighting comes from quadratic funding. FairShare adds deterministic onchain settlement, an explainable payout, a cap, and DAO membership read live from a Sputnik role.
  2. Collusion is made costly, not impossible. The Attack Lab shows a closed ring earning less. If the whole round colludes, the maths cannot save it.
  3. Popularity bias remains. Well liked members attract points. The cap and the square root soften it. They do not remove it.
  4. Sybil resistance comes from DAO member curation. The contract cannot tell a real member from a second account. The DAO decides who is a member.
  5. Membership in production is a Sputnik DAO role read across contracts. The contract mirrors the DAO council into its member set with sync_dao_members, and get_dao_role checks one account live against the DAO policy.
  6. Whole number division leaves a few yocto in the contract after each settle. That dust stays there.
  7. Sputnik decides approval by proposal kind and role, never by amount. A small proposal from the wrong role still fails, and a large proposal from the right role still passes.
  8. The contract cannot see physical work. It never claims to verify that a freezer was cleaned or a stall was run. It settles what members said about each other, nothing more.
