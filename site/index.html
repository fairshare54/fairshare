Peer allocation of credit

FairShare: ownership you earn, not buy

Bounties pay for work you can specify in advance. Communities run on work you cannot specify in advance: hosting, moderating, mentoring, showing up. FairShare lets a group fund that work together. A Sputnik DAO funds a round, members give each other points for the work they saw, and the contract settles payouts onchain, with every payout explainable.

Testnet only. This page runs in simulated demo mode until a real testnet contract is linked below. No wallet is connected here and no funds can move from this page.

How it works

  1. Shared budget. A Sputnik DAO funds a round with one envelope. In the demo the envelope is 10 NEAR.
  2. Peer allocation. Every member gets 100 points and gives them to at least 3 other members, with a short reason for each gift. You cannot point at yourself, and you cannot spend points you do not have. One submission per member; sending again replaces the old one.
  3. Settlement. The contract turns points into payouts. A score rewards breadth: points from many distinct givers count for more than the same points from one giver. Each giver's weight is their standing, which starts at 100 for everyone. Mutual giving is discounted by half, and nobody takes more than 25% of the envelope. A round settles only after 7 days, or after the owner closes it early.
  4. Explain. Anyone can ask the contract why a member was paid what they were paid, giver by giver, discount by discount.
  5. Ownership. Settling mints ownership units in proportion to each payout, and older units fade by 10% each round. When rental income lands in the treasury, distribute_inflow shares it out in proportion to units. Ownership grows out of work, not out of buying in.

Round rules: 100 points per member · at least 3 recipients · cap 25% of the envelope · reciprocity discount 50% · giver weight is standing, starting at 100 · round lasts 7 days unless the owner closes it early.

The live flow, when a contract is linked

  1. Connect a NEAR wallet. Testnet only. The wallet connection is not wired in this build, so this step cannot run from this page yet.
  2. Create and fund a round. A Sputnik DAO proposes a FunctionCall to create_round with the envelope attached. Only the owner or the DAO can call it. The DAO votes, and the proposal executes.
  3. Allocate. Each member calls allocate with their points and reasons. One submission per member; sending again replaces the old one.
  4. Settle. Anyone calls settle_round once the 7 day deadline passes or the owner closes the round early. The contract pays each member from the envelope and the round closes.

Honest limits

  1. Peer allocation is not new. Coordinape did a version of it, and square root weighting comes from quadratic funding. FairShare adds deterministic onchain settlement, an explainable payout, and a cap.
  2. Collusion is made costly, not impossible. The Attack Lab shows a closed ring earning less. If the whole round colludes, the maths cannot save it.
  3. Popularity bias remains. Well liked members attract points. The cap and the square root soften it. They do not remove it.
  4. Sybil resistance comes from DAO member curation. The contract cannot tell a real member from a second account. The DAO decides who is a member.
  5. Membership in production is a Sputnik DAO role read across contracts. The contract mirrors the DAO council into its member set with sync_dao_members, and get_dao_role checks one account live against the DAO policy.
  6. Whole number division leaves a few yocto in the contract after each settle. That dust stays there.
  7. Sputnik decides approval by proposal kind and role, never by amount. A small proposal from the wrong role still fails, and a large proposal from the right role still passes.
  8. The contract cannot see physical work. It never claims to verify that a freezer was cleaned or a stall was run. It settles what members said about each other, nothing more.
