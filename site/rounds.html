The funding proposal taught the project something real. It failed twice before it passed: the DAO looks rich on its total balance, but Sputnik stores every proposal onchain, so the DAO must keep about 4.74 NEAR staked for its own storage, and executing a proposal also returns the 1 NEAR bond. A 5 NEAR envelope therefore needs the DAO at about 11.5 NEAR total, not 5. The DAO was topped up to 11.5 NEAR and the seventh vote passed. The full story, with the failed votes, is in the repo runbook.

The DAO and governance

Membership in FairShare is a Sputnik DAO role, read live across contracts. The contract mirrors the DAO council into its member set with sync_dao_members, and get_dao_role checks one account live against the DAO policy. The DAO is the source of truth. The local member set is a synced mirror.

DAO: fairshare-dao.testnet · Name: fairshare · Council: 19 members, the 6 demo members plus the 6 round 2 crew plus the 6 round 4 crew plus clearsite587.testnet, who joined by proposal #15 · Policy: 1 of 2 vote to pass, 1 NEAR proposal bond, 7 day proposal period

DAO deploy: 23KcWfmQD2cu6nBJkx1yAoWk8istBR75omCzXVfZUDnv · DAO init: DV39wdiRmhgLAwZ2TCtZncR8RVMhQQ8iPy7jHd2YyYug

  1. Join. A new member joins the DAO council by proposal. The council votes, and the proposal executes. Sputnik decides approval by proposal kind and role, never by amount.
  2. Sync. Anyone calls sync_dao_members on the round contract. It reads the DAO get_policy live and mirrors the council into the member set.
  3. Fund. The DAO funds a round by proposing a FunctionCall to create_round with the envelope attached. The council votes, the proposal executes, and the envelope sits in escrow in the contract.
  4. Allocate and settle. Members allocate, the owner closes the round early or the 7 day deadline passes, and anyone calls settle_round. The contract pays each member from the envelope.

One fact worth knowing: the live testnet factory only creates DAOs as its own subaccounts, so it cannot create the top level name fairshare-dao.testnet. The real Sputnik v2 contract was deployed directly to that account instead, with the wasm read onchain from genesis.sputnikv2.testnet.

Security & Membership

Governance-Controlled Membership

Connecting a NEAR wallet does not automatically grant FairShare membership.

Every member must first be approved through a strict-majority Sputnik DAO proposal. Once approved, the council membership is synchronised with the FairShare contract, granting the wallet permission to participate in allocations.

When a member is removed from the DAO council, their FairShare allocation permission is revoked at the next successful membership synchronisation. The contract records the last synchronisation time.

FairShare uses governance-based Sybil resistance, not cryptographic identity verification. Multiple wallets belonging to one person could still be admitted if the council approves them.

Membership is governed by the community, not granted automatically by wallet connection.

Trezu manages this DAO

Trezu is the treasury app for Sputnik DAOs. This DAO is a standard Sputnik DAO, so it is the kind of DAO Trezu manages: council members propose, vote and execute through it, and the contract reads membership live from it.

The honest limit: Trezu production is mainnet only, so there is no live Trezu testnet payout, and none is claimed here. What was verified is the local testnet view: the Trezu frontend run locally with the mock backend from the Trezu repo, opening fairshare-dao.testnet, shows the real onchain data. Requests, Executed tab: all 16 proposals, each Executed, including the round 2, round 3, and round 4 funding proposals and the proposal that added clearsite587.testnet to the council. Members: all 19 council members. Settings, Voting: 10 of 19 threshold, 7 day vote duration. Assets and recent transactions stay empty, because there is no indexer for testnet DAOs. The exact steps and screenshots are in the repo, TREZU.md.
